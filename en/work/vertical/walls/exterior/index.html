    Exterior Walls¶

    What it is¶

    An exterior wall runs along the building perimeter and has sheathing on the outside. It differs from interior walls in its layers (sheathing, WRB, insulation), the FRT requirement and the fact that all facade openings are cut out of it. A wall on concrete brings along Sill Plates, and at the top Gable, Parapet and Truss Heel.

    Where to find it on the plans¶

    Sheet What to take
    Floor plans a101 1st Wall outline and length, wall type marks
    Wall type schedule (wt) Stud size, spacing, layers, fire rating, FRT
    Sections a3.01 sec Stud height, floor framing makeup, where the wall ends and the gable starts
    Elevations el, energy sheets Densglass and Zip zones, continuous insulation, material changes
    Structural s101, SW schedule Shear walls, holdowns, blocking of tall walls
    Code summary (n) Construction type: on Type III everything in the plane of the exterior wall is FRT

    How to measure in OurPlanCore¶

    Line tool, LFT unit. The W sheet dot on a floor plan puts a new line into walls/<floor> floor walls.

    1. Run the line along the wall centerline over the real runs. Do not trace callouts or dimension lines.
    2. Start a new takeoff when the height, size, construction or sheathed side changes. One run with several callouts stays one line.
    3. Decide the wall type run by run, not as a group: a piece of demising between two exterior walls does not become ext.
    4. A double wall is two frames only if the plan shows two frames.
    5. BW and shear walls are not drawn in walls: they have their own branches, BW and Shear Wall.
    6. Give each takeoff its own Color.

    Stud height¶

    The height in the name is the wall height including plates, in decimal feet. It is not floor-to-floor and not the ceiling elevation. Read the section of each building: from the floor-to-floor subtract the subfloor and the floor framing depth, then check whether the gypcrete is poured over the framing or between it. Example: 9'-6" floor-to-floor minus 11 7/8" TJI minus 3/4" subfloor = 8.45'. In the DFL the height lives in column E of the stud row (to 1/8"), in the wall name it is decimal feet.

    Which stud for the wall height¶

    The wall height (with plates, as in the name) decides which stud to order:

    Wall height Stud
    7'-0" ... 8'-1 5/8" 8' precut (92-5/8)
    8'-1 3/4" ... 8'-4 1/2" 8' full height stud
    8'-4 3/4" ... 9'-1 5/8" 9' precut (104-5/8)
    9'-1 3/4" ... 10'-1 5/8" 10' precut (116-5/8)
    10'-1 3/4" ... 10'-4 1/2" 10' full height stud
    10'-4 3/4" ... 12'-4 1/2" 12'
    • There is no 9' full height stud, and no 11' stud either.
    • 10' precut is the tallest precut.
    • Short studs are cut from long ones: count 4' as 8', 5' as 10', 6' as 12'.
    • Example: an 8'-4 1/2" wall takes an 8' full height stud, a 12'-2" wall takes a 12' stud. Gables are not always 12' studs: check the height of each stud.

    Takeoff name and DFL row¶

    Naming canon: <category> <size> <height>. Older examples with x (ext 2x6 x) mean the same thing: the x stands in place of the height.

    Takeoff name What it is DFL row Unit
    ext 2x6 9.09 Exterior 2x6, 9.09' high Studs Ext, Plates Ext (1) btm P.T., Plates Ext (2) dbl top pcs, LFT
    corr 2x6 11.7 Corridor wall, see Corridor Studs Corr, Plates Corr pcs, LFT
    dem 2x6 9.09 Unit separation wall, see Demising Studs Demising, Plates Demising pcs, LFT
    dem (2) 2x6 9.09 Double demising, two identical frames two groups of rows pcs, LFT
    furr 2x4 9.09 Furring wall, see Furring Plates Furring, Furring LFT
    2x4 9.09 Regular partition, no prefix Studs Interior, Plates Interior pcs, LFT
    2x6 half Half wall height from the name pcs, LFT

    Along with the wall the DFL gets Bracing Ext Walls 2x4, Blocking, Wall Sheathing, Vapor Barrier (the template label; the material is the WRB from the spec, not Tyvek by default), Insulation, Holdowns and Ext. Headers. Stud factors (16" = 1.1, 24" = 0.625) have one canon in Formulas. The WL button on the XL strip moves the walls into the floor block; a suffix after the height (ext 2x6 10.09 5/S400) is split off automatically.

    Layer rules are on separate pages: Wall Sheathing, Exterior Wall Materials. FRT: if the exterior wall is FRT, then the blocking, the parapet and the headers in its plane are FRT too. Count a facade build-out separately for the vertical and the horizontal parts, FRT per detail. Metal stud and CMU walls: framing is by others, but exterior gypsum, insulation, flashing and window jambs stay ours (Metal Tracks).

    Common mistakes¶

    • Stud height taken as floor-to-floor or the ceiling elevation.
    • Wall type assigned as a group: corridor and demising mixed up, a piece of demising ended up in exterior.
    • P.T. plate on wood floor framing: P.T. only on concrete and masonry.
    • Double bottom plate everywhere although the detail shows it only on some floors.
    • The barrier row says Tyvek with a fluid-applied WRB: use the product from the spec.
    • Openings subtracted from the sheathing twice or not at all.
    • Missed rigid insulation, Densglass by facade zone, dropped soffit from the RCP.
